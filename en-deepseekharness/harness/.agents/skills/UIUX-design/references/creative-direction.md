# Find a Creative Direction in the Product's Content

**Find one detail specific to the product.** Look at what users do before and after opening the screen. Look for the language, objects, habits, or working conditions they actually encounter. Choose a detail that can influence the structure. If users return every day to review the paragraph where they stopped, show that paragraph and its annotations in the first view. Prefer authorized photos of actual objects, product content, or reliable records. Text and graphics can tell the story when those materials are unavailable. Indicate the purpose and provenance of sample materials.

**Describe what works about a reference.** In a paper notebook with annotations, the useful idea may be the proximity of the passage to its notes. Translate that relationship into two columns on desktop and inline expansion on small screens. Make your own choices about texture, color, type, and decorative lines. Explain what problem the reference solved, which relationship you adopted, and what changed for this project. When the asset index lacks a suitable reference, develop a direction from the user's task.

**Express the direction in the screen itself.** In a work area, show the object the user needs to work on first. On an introductory page, let footage of the product in use, a photo from the actual setting, or a substantiated fact lead the first view. Make one clear design choice for the page and use the rest of the space to support reading and action. Check the purpose of jargon-heavy slogans, fabricated testimonials, decorative numbering, and repeated headings. Keep number-based forms where they convey real data or a genuine sequence.

**Hold content constant when comparing options.** Create two meaningfully different examples with the same task, copy, and data. One might support processing items in queue order, while another emphasizes the details of one item. Explain what each makes easier or harder, and let the user choose. Try substituting another product's name, hiding the logo, or returning to a common template. Check which useful characteristics disappear. If the remaining difference is color alone, revisit the content and layout. For local improvements, preserve the interaction positions users already know.
