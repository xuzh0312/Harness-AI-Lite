## Overall Page Layout Patterns

> The underlying page structure determines how content is organized.

### 1.1 Single-Page Layout
- **Definition**: All content lives on one page; anchors or scroll navigation move users between sections without loading another page.
- **Structure**: Vertically stacked sections with anchor links that scroll to each one.
- **Motion**: Smooth scrolling, section entrances, and scroll-driven storytelling.
- **Use cases**: Company sites, product overviews, event pages, personal sites, and SaaS landing pages.
- **Implementation**: Anchor navigation with Lenis for smooth scrolling and GSAP ScrollTrigger.
- **Examples**: Apple product pages, SaaS sites, and portfolios.
- **Keywords**: single page, anchors, long scroll, landing page

### 1.2 Multi-Page Layout
- **Definition**: Content is split across separate pages linked by navigation.
- **Structure**: Separate HTML pages share a navigation bar and footer.
- **Motion**: Page transitions and breadcrumb navigation.
- **Use cases**: Company sites, news portals, e-commerce, and admin systems.
- **Implementation**: Traditional multi-page rendering, SSR, or Next.js page routing.
- **Examples**: Sina, Taobao, company sites, and news sites.
- **Keywords**: multi-page, page transitions, routing, website

### 1.3 Long-Scroll Layout
- **Definition**: An extended single page uses scrolling to set the pace of exploration.
- **Structure**: Long vertical content divided into sections.
- **Motion**: Scroll-driven storytelling, parallax, entrance animations, and progress bars.
- **Use cases**: Brand stories, product showcases, data journalism, and portfolios.
- **Implementation**: ScrollTrigger, Lenis, and parallax effects.
- **Examples**: Apple launch pages, New York Times features, and award-winning sites.
- **Keywords**: long scroll, vertical flow, storytelling, immersion

### 1.4 Hero-Focused Layout
- **Definition**: The above-the-fold hero dominates the viewport and concentrates attention on a single message.
- **Structure**: Large imagery or type in the hero, with other content secondary.
- **Motion**: Hero entrance animations and animated backgrounds.
- **Use cases**: Homepages, product landing pages, and event pages.
- **Implementation**: `min-height: 100vh` plus a hero animation.
- **Examples**: The hero sections of most official websites.
- **Keywords**: hero, above the fold, focus, 100vh

### 1.5 Split-Screen Layout
- **Definition**: Two equal halves, side by side or stacked, carry different content.
- **Structure**: Symmetrical partitions and two content areas.
- **Motion**: Staggered entrances and panels that expand on hover.
- **Use cases**: Two-product comparisons, sign-in and sign-up pages, and creative sites.
- **Implementation**: An even CSS Grid or Flexbox split that stacks on smaller screens.
- **Examples**: Creative sites, product comparisons, and sign-in pages with two entry points.
- **Keywords**: split screen, equal halves, two columns, comparison

### 1.6 Fullscreen Background Layout
- **Definition**: A fullscreen image or video serves as the backdrop for overlaid content.
- **Structure**: Fullscreen background with a foreground content layer.
- **Motion**: Ken Burns zoom, looping video, and parallax.
- **Use cases**: Hero sections, brand and event sites, travel, and photography.
- **Implementation**: A fullscreen `<img>/<video>` with an overlay.
- **Examples**: Travel and hotel sites and photography portfolios.
- **Keywords**: fullscreen background, video background, overlay, atmosphere

### 1.7 Magazine Layout
- **Definition**: Varied combinations of text and images evoke a print magazine, with modules of different sizes creating visual rhythm.
- **Structure**: Mixed text and imagery, varied module sizes, and lead images paired with summaries.
- **Motion**: Staggered module entrances and hover previews.
- **Use cases**: Portals and news, fashion, and media sites.
- **Implementation**: A mixed CSS Grid with image-and-text modules.
- **Examples**: The New York Times, Vogue, and fashion media homepages.
- **Keywords**: magazine, editorial layout, mixed media, portal

### 1.8 Grid Layout
- **Definition**: A regular arrangement of rows and columns gives content a clear sense of order.
- **Structure**: Consistent rows, columns, and alignment.
- **Motion**: Card entrances and grid reflow.
- **Use cases**: Portfolios, image galleries, product listings, and dashboards.
- **Implementation**: CSS Grid or Bootstrap Grid.
- **Examples**: Unsplash, some Pinterest views, and portfolios.
- **Keywords**: grid, rows and columns, regularity, alignment

### 1.9 Card-Based Layout
- **Definition**: Individual cards group related information into clearly bounded, scannable units.
- **Structure**: Rounded cards, shadows, and even spacing.
- **Motion**: Card hover, expansion, and flip effects.
- **Use cases**: Content aggregation, e-commerce, news, and settings pages.
- **Implementation**: Flexbox or Grid with card components.
- **Examples**: Pinterest, Airbnb, and various feeds.
- **Keywords**: cards, aggregation, modularity, scanning

### 1.10 Masonry Layout
- **Definition**: Columns pack items of varying heights tightly without forcing them into aligned rows.
- **Structure**: Staggered columns and compact spacing.
- **Motion**: Loading on scroll and staggered card entrances.
- **Use cases**: Image communities, galleries, and e-commerce product walls.
- **Implementation**: CSS columns or a JavaScript masonry library such as Masonry.js.
- **Examples**: Pinterest, Huaban, and image sites.
- **Keywords**: masonry, image wall, staggered, multi-column

### 1.11 Bento Grid
- **Definition**: Rounded cards of different sizes fit together like compartments in a Japanese bento box: orderly but playful.
- **Structure**: Modular, rounded cards of varying sizes.
- **Motion**: Card entrances and expansion on hover.
- **Use cases**: Feature showcases, portfolios, data summaries, and app overviews.
- **Implementation**: CSS Grid with rounded cards that can span rows and columns.
- **Examples**: Apple Keynote pages, product sites, and personal sites.
- **Keywords**: Bento Grid, modularity, mixed sizes, rounded corners

### 1.12 Layered Parallax Layout
- **Definition**: Foreground, middle-ground, and background layers scroll at different speeds to create depth.
- **Structure**: Multiple content layers move at different speeds.
- **Motion**: Scroll offsets, depth, and parallax.
- **Use cases**: Brand sites, narrative pages, and game sites.
- **Implementation**: Scroll-driven GSAP transforms applied to multiple layers.
- **Examples**: Apple product pages, game sites, and award-winning work.
- **Keywords**: parallax, layers, depth, offset

## Reading Path Layout Patterns

> Layouts based on common scanning patterns help readers find and absorb information more quickly.

### 2.1 F-Pattern Layout
- **Definition**: Readers scan across the top first, then down the left, making progressively shorter horizontal passes.
- **Structure**: Key information sits toward the top and left, with the left edge as the visual axis.
- **Motion**: Motion is usually restrained to preserve the content hierarchy.
- **Use cases**: Articles, search results, news lists, and documentation.
- **Implementation**: Left-aligned content and a clear typographic hierarchy.
- **Examples**: Google Search, news lists, and technical documentation.
- **Keywords**: F-pattern, eye movement, scanning, left alignment

### 2.2 Z-Pattern Layout
- **Definition**: The eye moves in a Z: top left → top right → bottom left → bottom right.
- **Structure**: Logo at the top left, main content in the middle, and action button at the bottom right.
- **Motion**: Motion draws attention to the primary CTA.
- **Use cases**: Landing pages, hero sections, and marketing pages with sparse content.
- **Implementation**: A diagonal visual path paired with deliberate CTA placement.
- **Examples**: Simple marketing landing pages and brand homepages.
- **Keywords**: Z-pattern, eye movement, diagonal, CTA

### 2.3 Gutenberg Diagram
- **Definition**: Describes how the eye moves across an evenly distributed page: top left → top right → bottom left → bottom right, forming four zones of attention.
- **Structure**: The top-left primary optical area and bottom-right terminal area receive the most emphasis.
- **Motion**: Motion guides the eye from the top left to the bottom right.
- **Use cases**: Balanced information pages, posters, ads, and brand pages.
- **Implementation**: Place the main visual emphasis in the top left and bottom right.
- **Examples**: Ads, posters, and evenly balanced pages.
- **Keywords**: Gutenberg diagram, reading path, attention zones, quadrants

### 2.4 Symmetrical Layout
- **Definition**: Mirrored composition around a horizontal or vertical axis conveys stability, formality, and dignity.
- **Structure**: Mirrored sides and a centered axis.
- **Motion**: Symmetrical reveals and emphasis at the center.
- **Use cases**: Premium brands, wedding photography, luxury, and formal corporate sites.
- **Implementation**: CSS centering and a mirrored structure.
- **Examples**: Luxury brand sites, wedding photography, and formal corporate sites.
- **Keywords**: symmetry, centering, dignity, formality

### 2.5 Asymmetrical Layout (Broken Grid)
- **Definition**: Deliberate breaks in symmetry and grid alignment use offsets and overlap to create tension and character.
- **Structure**: Asymmetry, offsets, overlaps, and contrasting amounts of whitespace.
- **Motion**: Offset slide animations and interactions between overlapping elements.
- **Use cases**: Creative portfolios, fashion brands, and art websites.
- **Implementation**: Negative margins, overlaps, and offset Grid placement.
- **Examples**: Fashion brand sites, creative studios, and designer portfolios.
- **Keywords**: asymmetry, broken grid, offsets, overlap, tension

## Navigation Layout Patterns

> A core part of layout design: how users move through a site or app and understand where they are.

### 3.1 Top Navigation
- **Definition**: A horizontal navigation bar at the top of the page contains the logo, menu, and actions.
- **Structure**: A persistently visible horizontal top bar.
- **Motion**: Shrinking on scroll, sticky positioning, and dropdown menus.
- **Use cases**: Official sites, corporate sites, and web apps.
- **Implementation**: CSS sticky or fixed positioning with Flexbox.
- **Examples**: The top navigation on most websites.
- **Keywords**: top navigation, sticky header, horizontal, persistent

### 3.2 Bottom Tab Bar
- **Definition**: A horizontal bar of tabs fixed to the bottom of the screen, common on mobile.
- **Structure**: Three to five bottom tabs with icons and labels.
- **Motion**: Tab transitions, hiding on scroll, and bouncing icons.
- **Use cases**: Primary navigation in mobile apps and mini programs.
- **Implementation**: A fixed bottom bar with tab components.
- **Examples**: WeChat, Douyin, Taobao, and iOS apps.
- **Keywords**: bottom tab bar, mobile, primary navigation

### 3.3 Drawer Navigation (Hamburger Menu)
- **Definition**: Navigation stays hidden until a tap on the hamburger icon opens a panel from the side.
- **Structure**: A slide-out panel and hamburger button.
- **Motion**: The drawer slides in and out while the backdrop fades.
- **Use cases**: Mobile screens, space-constrained interfaces, and side menus.
- **Implementation**: CSS transforms for the slide, plus a backdrop.
- **Examples**: Mobile menus and the Material Design navigation drawer.
- **Keywords**: drawer, hamburger menu, slide-out, navigation

### 3.4 Sidebar Layout
- **Definition**: A persistent vertical navigation or information panel on the left or right of the page.
- **Structure**: Typically a vertical menu on the left and content on the right.
- **Motion**: Collapsing and expanding the sidebar and updating active-item highlights.
- **Use cases**: Admin tools, documentation, desktop apps, and blogs.
- **Implementation**: Flexbox with a fixed-width sidebar and a flexible content area.
- **Examples**: Notion, Yuque, admin systems, and documentation sites.
- **Keywords**: sidebar, left navigation, collapse, admin

### 3.5 Top Tabs
- **Definition**: A horizontal row of tabs at the top switches between peer content areas.
- **Structure**: Horizontal tabs coordinated with the content panel.
- **Motion**: Sliding active-tab indicator and crossfades between panels.
- **Use cases**: Content categories, channels, libraries, and app category screens.
- **Implementation**: Tab components with a sliding indicator.
- **Examples**: News channels, video categories, and settings pages.
- **Keywords**: tabs, categories, sliding indicator, switching

### 3.6 List Navigation
- **Definition**: Navigation options are arranged in a vertical list, often for settings, menus, or directories.
- **Structure**: Vertical rows with an icon, label, and arrow.
- **Motion**: Tap feedback on rows and expandable subitems.
- **Use cases**: Settings, menus, directories, and account screens.
- **Implementation**: Grouped list components.
- **Examples**: iOS Settings and various menu lists.
- **Keywords**: list, vertical navigation, settings, menu

### 3.7 Dashboard Grid Navigation
- **Definition**: A grid of icons, often 3 × 3, provides shortcuts to features.
- **Structure**: Icon-based entry points aligned in a grid.
- **Motion**: Hover states, bouncing shortcuts, and drag-to-reorder interactions.
- **Use cases**: App home screens, admin homepages, and portals.
- **Implementation**: CSS Grid with icon components.
- **Examples**: Alipay’s home screen, admin workspaces, and portal navigation.
- **Keywords**: dashboard grid, 3 × 3 grid, shortcuts, icon grid

### 3.8 Command Palette
- **Definition**: A keyboard shortcut opens a global search and command panel for quick, mouse-free actions.
- **Structure**: Centered overlay with a search field and command list.
- **Motion**: Panel entrance, live filtering, and result highlighting.
- **Use cases**: Productivity tools, editors, and developer tools.
- **Implementation**: A global shortcut, an overlay, and fuzzy search.
- **Examples**: Raycast, VS Code, Notion, and Slack.
- **Keywords**: command palette, keyboard shortcut, search, productivity

### 3.9 Breadcrumb Navigation
- **Definition**: Shows the current page’s place in a hierarchy and lets users return to each ancestor.
- **Structure**: A linked hierarchy with separators such as `>` or `·`.
- **Motion**: Usually static, with hover feedback.
- **Use cases**: E-commerce, documentation, admin tools, and deeply nested sites.
- **Implementation**: A list with styled separators.
- **Examples**: Taobao, e-commerce categories, documentation sites, and admin systems.
- **Keywords**: breadcrumbs, hierarchy, path, backtracking

## Homepage and Landing Page Layouts

> Homepage and landing page patterns designed to support conversion.

### 4.1 Centered Hero
- **Definition**: The hero headline, subheading, and CTA are centered.
- **Structure**: Centered copy and buttons.
- **Motion**: Copy fades in and rises; buttons receive visual emphasis.
- **Use cases**: SaaS, app downloads, and brand homepages.
- **Implementation**: Flexbox centering and hero animations.
- **Examples**: SaaS websites and app landing pages.
- **Keywords**: centered hero, above the fold, CTA, SaaS

### 4.2 Split Hero: Text Left, Image Right
- **Definition**: A classic two-column hero with copy on the left and a product image or illustration on the right.
- **Structure**: Two columns: copy on the left, visuals on the right.
- **Motion**: Staggered text and image entrances and animated imagery.
- **Use cases**: Product overviews, app landing pages, and SaaS.
- **Implementation**: Two-column CSS Grid that stacks on smaller screens.
- **Examples**: Slack, Dropbox, and SaaS homepages.
- **Keywords**: split hero, two columns, product image, landing page

### 4.3 Three-Part Layout (Hero – Features – CTA)
- **Definition**: A classic landing-page sequence: hero introduction → features or selling points → call to action.
- **Structure**: Three vertically stacked sections build on one another.
- **Motion**: Section entrances and scroll cues.
- **Use cases**: SaaS, app, and event landing pages.
- **Implementation**: Stacked sections with scroll animations.
- **Examples**: Most landing pages.
- **Keywords**: three-part layout, features, CTA, landing page, progression

### 4.4 Pricing Page Layout
- **Definition**: Side-by-side plan cards allow comparison and highlight a recommended tier.
- **Structure**: Parallel cards, one emphasized recommendation, and comparable prices.
- **Motion**: Card hover, emphasis on the recommended plan, and billing-cycle toggles.
- **Use cases**: SaaS, subscription services, and membership products.
- **Implementation**: A card grid, emphasis styles, and a toggle.
- **Examples**: Notion and other SaaS pricing pages.
- **Keywords**: pricing, plans, comparison, subscription

### 4.5 Case Study / Portfolio Layout
- **Definition**: Project cards showcase work and link to further details.
- **Structure**: A grid or list of cards, each with a thumbnail and title.
- **Motion**: Card entrances, hover previews, and zoom transitions.
- **Use cases**: Design firms, studios, personal portfolios, and B2B sites.
- **Implementation**: Grid or list layouts with hover effects and detail pages.
- **Examples**: Design studio sites and Awwwards portfolios.
- **Keywords**: case study, portfolio, showcase

## Content Page Layouts

> Page structures for presenting specific types of content.

### 5.1 Single-Column Article / Blog Layout
- **Definition**: A centered text column and generous side margins keep attention on reading.
- **Structure**: A narrow centered column, ample whitespace, and continuous text flow.
- **Motion**: Reading progress bars and lazy-loaded images.
- **Use cases**: Blogs, long-form articles, publishing platforms, and news articles.
- **Implementation**: A narrow column constrained by `max-width` and centered.
- **Examples**: Medium, Zhihu columns, and personal blogs.
- **Keywords**: single column, blog, body text, reading, narrow column

### 5.2 Two-Column Article / Blog Layout (Content + Sidebar)
- **Definition**: A main text column sits beside a sidebar for a table of contents, recommendations, or ads.
- **Structure**: Main content on the left and sidebar on the right, or the reverse.
- **Motion**: Active table-of-contents links track scrolling; the sidebar sticks in view.
- **Use cases**: Blogs, news sites, and tutorial sites.
- **Implementation**: A two-column grid with a wide main area and a narrow sidebar.
- **Examples**: Technical blogs and news sites.
- **Keywords**: two columns, sidebar, blog, table of contents, news

### 5.3 Three-Column Content Layout (Content + Two Sidebars)
- **Definition**: The main content is centered between two sidebars for navigation, ads, or tools.
- **Structure**: A narrow center column flanked by two wide sidebars.
- **Motion**: Sticky sidebars and panels that update with the content.
- **Use cases**: Portals, forums, and utility or news sites.
- **Implementation**: Three-column Grid: sidebar, main content, sidebar.
- **Examples**: Portals, forums, and older news sites.
- **Keywords**: three columns, dual sidebars, portal, forum, center column

### 5.4 Product Detail Page Layout
- **Definition**: A product detail page pairs images on the left with information on the right, or stacks the two vertically.
- **Structure**: Image area and product information area for name, price, options, and purchase.
- **Motion**: Image switching, magnification, and add-to-cart feedback.
- **Use cases**: E-commerce, SaaS product details, and app listings.
- **Implementation**: Two columns, an image carousel, and a purchase button.
- **Examples**: Taobao, JD.com, and App Store detail pages.
- **Keywords**: product details, e-commerce, purchase, specifications

### 5.5 Search Results Page Layout
- **Definition**: Search results appear in a list or cards, with a search box and filters above.
- **Structure**: Search bar, filters, and result list.
- **Motion**: Results fade in, filters update results, and more items load on demand.
- **Use cases**: E-commerce, search engines, content platforms, and admin search.
- **Implementation**: An F-pattern list with filter components.
- **Examples**: Google, Taobao search, and content site search.
- **Keywords**: search results, filters, list, retrieval

### 5.6 About / Team Page Layout
- **Definition**: Introduces a company or team through its mission, culture, and people.
- **Structure**: Brand story, team grid, and key figures.
- **Motion**: Team-card entrances and animated counters.
- **Use cases**: Corporate sites, startups, and studios.
- **Implementation**: Narrative sections and a grid of team members.
- **Examples**: Company About Us pages.
- **Keywords**: about us, team, people, brand story

### 5.7 Contact / Form Page Layout
- **Definition**: Combines contact information with a submission form.
- **Structure**: Contact details and a form, either side by side or centered.
- **Motion**: Validation feedback and a submission success animation.
- **Use cases**: Corporate sites, business inquiries, and registrations.
- **Implementation**: Form components, validation, and submission feedback.
- **Examples**: Corporate Contact Us pages and registration pages.
- **Keywords**: contact, form, validation, submission

## Data and Dashboard Layouts

> Layouts for data-intensive interfaces.

### 6.1 Dashboard Layout
- **Definition**: A data panel groups key metrics into reusable cards and chart modules.
- **Structure**: A modular arrangement of metric cards and charts.
- **Motion**: Chart animations, animated counters, and card entrances.
- **Use cases**: BI tools, operations consoles, and monitoring centers.
- **Implementation**: Bento Grid with chart components such as ECharts or Recharts.
- **Examples**: Grafana, BI tools, and admin workspaces.
- **Keywords**: dashboard, metrics, charts, data panel

### 6.2 Large-Screen Data Display Layout
- **Definition**: A high-density, visually striking layout for presenting data on a large display.
- **Structure**: Fullscreen modular grid, dark background, and neon or tech-inspired styling.
- **Motion**: Live data refreshes, sweeping highlights, and chart animations.
- **Use cases**: Command centers, showrooms, monitoring displays, and data presentations.
- **Implementation**: ECharts, a fullscreen grid, and live WebSocket updates.
- **Examples**: Smart-city dashboards, monitoring centers, and command rooms.
- **Keywords**: large-screen dashboard, visualization, real-time, tech styling

### 6.3 Admin Layout
- **Definition**: A classic three-part admin shell: top bar, sidebar, and content area.
- **Structure**: Top bar, navigation on the left, and content on the right.
- **Motion**: Sidebar collapse, menu expansion, and content transitions.
- **Use cases**: Enterprise admin tools, CMSs, and management systems.
- **Implementation**: Admin frameworks such as Ant Design, Element Plus, or shadcn.
- **Examples**: Admin systems and CMSs.
- **Keywords**: admin, sidebar, top bar, management system

## Responsive Layouts and Grid Systems

> The underlying systems that help layouts adapt and remain consistent across devices.

### 7.1 12-Column Grid
- **Definition**: A standard grid divides the width into 12 equal columns and lets elements span anywhere from 1 to 12.
- **Structure**: Twelve equal columns with gutters and flexible spans.
- **Motion**: Grid reflow and layout changes at responsive breakpoints.
- **Use cases**: Nearly any web design.
- **Implementation**: CSS Grid, Bootstrap, or Ant Design Grid.
- **Examples**: Bootstrap, Ant Design, and other design systems.
- **Keywords**: 12-column grid, columns, gutters, design system

### 7.2 Mobile First
- **Definition**: Design for mobile first, then progressively enhance the layout for larger screens.
- **Structure**: The layout gains features in stages from small screens to desktop.
- **Motion**: Smooth transitions between breakpoint layouts.
- **Use cases**: Mobile-led products and responsive websites.
- **Implementation**: Progressive CSS enhancements using `min-width` media queries.
- **Examples**: Modern mobile-first sites.
- **Keywords**: mobile first, progressive enhancement, responsive design

### 7.3 Responsive Breakpoints
- **Definition**: Screen-width thresholds trigger layout changes.
- **Structure**: Several breakpoints for phones, tablets, and desktops.
- **Motion**: Layout reflow when crossing a breakpoint.
- **Use cases**: Any responsive layout.
- **Implementation**: `@media` queries, for example at 576, 768, 992, and 1200px.
- **Examples**: Bootstrap’s breakpoint system.
- **Keywords**: breakpoints, media queries, responsive design, screen size

### 7.4 Adaptive vs. Responsive Layout
- **Definition**: Adaptive designs use multiple fixed layouts for specific devices; responsive designs use one fluid layout that adjusts continuously.
- **Structure**: Preset fixed layouts versus continuously resizing elements.
- **Motion**: Fluid responsive layouts can provide smoother transitions.
- **Use cases**: Adaptive fits complex desktop apps; responsive fits content sites.
- **Implementation**: Fixed layouts at selected breakpoints, or fluid sizing with media queries.
- **Examples**: Some admin interfaces use adaptive layouts; mainstream sites often use responsive ones.
- **Keywords**: adaptive, responsive, fluid layout

### 7.5 Flexbox Layout
- **Definition**: A one-dimensional layout model that flexibly arranges and aligns items along main and cross axes.
- **Structure**: Flexible rows or columns, centering, and equal distribution.
- **Motion**: Flex transitions and item reflow.
- **Use cases**: Navigation, card interiors, centered elements, and toolbars.
- **Implementation**: CSS `display: flex` with alignment properties.
- **Examples**: Nearly all modern layouts.
- **Keywords**: Flexbox, one-dimensional layout, alignment, centering

### 7.6 Fluid Layout
- **Definition**: Percentages or relative units let widths change smoothly with the viewport.
- **Structure**: Widths are set relative to the container.
- **Motion**: Continuous resizing as the viewport changes.
- **Use cases**: Responsive sites and flowing content.
- **Implementation**: Percentages, `max-width`, and `clamp()`.
- **Examples**: Modern responsive websites.
- **Keywords**: fluid layout, percentages, scaling, clamp

### 7.7 Fixed-Width Layout
- **Definition**: The page’s content area has a fixed pixel width regardless of the viewport.
- **Structure**: A centered, fixed-width container.
- **Motion**: No responsive layout changes.
- **Use cases**: Legacy websites, specialized desktop tools, and email templates.
- **Implementation**: Fixed `width` with `margin: auto`.
- **Examples**: Older websites and some email templates.
- **Keywords**: fixed width, desktop, centering, legacy

## Appendix: Layout Decision Guide

### A. Choose a Layout by Product Type

| Product type | Recommended layout |
|---|---|
| SaaS landing page | Centered or split hero (text left, image right) + three-part layout (hero, features, CTA) + pricing page |
| Corporate website | Top navigation + multi-page structure + About Us and Contact Us pages |
| E-commerce | Cards + masonry grid + product detail pages + search filters |
| Content site or blog | One or two columns (content + sidebar) + F-pattern reading |
| Mobile app | Bottom tab bar + top tabs + list or 3 × 3 grid |
| Admin system | Sidebar + top bar + content area (three-part admin shell) |
| Large-screen data display | Fullscreen multi-module grid + dark, tech-inspired styling |
| Portfolio | Bento Grid, masonry, or a broken grid |

### B. Common Grid and Breakpoint Reference

| Breakpoint | Screen | Typical column count |
|---|---|---|
| < 576px | Phone | 1–4 columns |
| ≥ 576px | Large phone | 4–8 columns |
| ≥ 768px | Tablet | 8–12 columns |
| ≥ 992px | Small desktop | 12 columns |
| ≥ 1200px | Large desktop | 12 columns (wide container) |
