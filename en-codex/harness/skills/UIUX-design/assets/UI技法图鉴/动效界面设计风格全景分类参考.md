## Materials and Morphism

> Light, blur, and shadow evoke the textures of real materials and make an interface feel tactile.

### 1.1 Flat Design
- **Definition**: A minimalist 2D style that strips away shadows, gradients, and textures in response to skeuomorphism.
- **Visual characteristics**: Solid colors, no shadows, clear typography, and simplified icons.
- **Motion**: Restrained transitions and simple movement.
- **Use cases**: Functional admin tools, company sites, utilities, and large data displays.
- **Implementation**: CSS alone; no special dependencies.
- **Examples**: Early iOS 7, Windows 8 and 10, and early Google Material Design.
- **Keywords**: flat design, minimalism, 2D

### 1.2 Skeuomorphism
- **Definition**: Interface elements imitate the materials, textures, and lighting of real objects, such as leather, metal, or paper.
- **Visual characteristics**: Realistic textures, rich shadows, and dimensional layers.
- **Motion**: Press and rebound, page turns, and lifelike physical feedback.
- **Use cases**: Nostalgic products, game UI, e-readers, and isolated realistic details.
- **Implementation**: Bitmap assets with layered shadows or gradients.
- **Examples**: Early iOS (versions 1–6), classic calculators, and the iBooks bookshelf.
- **Keywords**: skeuomorphism, realistic textures, physical materials

### 1.3 Neumorphism (Soft UI)
- **Definition**: A pair of soft, same-hue shadows—one light and one dark—makes an element appear raised from or recessed into its background.
- **Visual characteristics**: Low contrast, colors close to the background, and subtly lit edges.
- **Motion**: Shadows reverse on press, making the control appear to sink.
- **Use cases**: Small controls such as switches, sliders, buttons, and volume dials. Large areas of this treatment can become hard to read.
- **Implementation**: Two `box-shadow` layers: light on the upper left, dark on the lower right.
- **Examples**: Dribbble concepts and some smart-home app controls.
- **Keywords**: neumorphism, soft UI, dual shadows

### 1.4 Glassmorphism
- **Definition**: Backdrop blur and translucent overlays create a layered effect reminiscent of floating frosted glass.
- **Visual characteristics**: Blurred backdrops, translucent white outlines, subtle highlights, and transparency.
- **Motion**: Floating, scaling, and transitions that reveal the background through the surface.
- **Use cases**: Dashboards, floating navigation, dialogs, cards, sidebars, and sign-in pages.
- **Implementation**: CSS `backdrop-filter: blur()` with translucent borders and several z-index layers.
- **Examples**: macOS Notification Center, iOS Control Center, and macOS Big Sur.
- **Keywords**: glassmorphism, frosted glass, backdrop-filter

### 1.5 Liquid Glass
- **Definition**: Apple introduced this design language at WWDC25 in June 2025. Its translucent, adaptive material refracts, reflects, and concentrates light in real time, while controls change shape with interaction.
- **Visual characteristics**: Live refractions and reflections, adaptation to content and light or dark appearance, curves that echo the screen corners, and a fully transparent mode.
- **Motion**: Controls morph on touch or scroll, menus emerge, playback controls respond to the interface, and tab bars shrink or expand as users scroll.
- **Use cases**: App navigation, floating toolbars, pop-up menus, playback controls, and system interfaces.
- **Implementation**: Native components in the iOS 26 SDK and SwiftUI; custom approximations can combine `backdrop-filter`, dynamic highlights, spring motion, and real-time rendering.
- **Examples**: System interfaces in iOS 26, iPadOS 26, macOS Tahoe 26, and watchOS 26.
- **Keywords**: Liquid Glass, Apple, real-time rendering, fluid materials, transparency

### 1.6 Claymorphism
- **Definition**: A soft, rounded 3D clay look with gentle inner shadows and highlights around the edges.
- **Visual characteristics**: Generous corner radii, soft inset shadows, creamy colors, and playful forms.
- **Motion**: Bouncy scaling, gentle rebounds, and squash effects.
- **Use cases**: Onboarding illustrations, education and children’s products, entertainment, mascots, icons, and empty states.
- **Implementation**: CSS inset shadows and rim highlights, or modeling in Figma or Spline.
- **Examples**: Sites with 3D illustrations and some SaaS onboarding flows.
- **Keywords**: claymorphism, rounded forms, playful, 3D illustration

### 1.7 Auromorphism
- **Definition**: Aurora gradients combine with material effects to create a dreamy atmosphere through softly colored halos.
- **Visual characteristics**: Multicolored diffuse light, hazy halos, and soft edges.
- **Motion**: Halos slowly drift and pulse.
- **Use cases**: Brand atmospheres, tech-inspired sign-in pages, and creative product heroes.
- **Implementation**: Multiple radial gradients with `filter: blur()` and translation animations.
- **Examples**: AI product sites and creative studio homepages.
- **Keywords**: auromorphism, aurora, halos, dreamy, gradients

### 1.8 Chrome Morphism
- **Definition**: Mirror-like highlights emulate liquid chrome for a futuristic finish.
- **Visual characteristics**: Specular reflections, silver or colored metallic gradients, and bright highlights.
- **Motion**: Sweeping highlights and shifting reflections.
- **Use cases**: Tech brands, game interfaces, designer collectibles, and Web3 or cryptocurrency interfaces.
- **Implementation**: Layered gradients, highlight masks, and animation.
- **Examples**: Web3 sites, esports products, and trend-focused brands.
- **Keywords**: chrome, metal, metallic, futuristic

### 1.9 Material Simulation
- **Definition**: Recreates real-world surfaces such as wood, paper, fabric, concrete, and plastic.
- **Visual characteristics**: Realistic textures and contrasts between materials.
- **Motion**: Feedback specific to the material, such as page turning, elasticity, or friction.
- **Use cases**: Brand sites, e-commerce, reading apps, and premium design portfolios.
- **Implementation**: Bitmap textures, lighting and shadows, and blend modes.
- **Examples**: Paper-textured reading apps, wood-grain storefronts, and concrete-inspired sites.
- **Keywords**: materials, texture, simulation, tactility

## Light and Color

> Light, color, gradients, and grain set the mood.

### 2.1 Aurora Gradient
- **Definition**: A background of slowly moving multicolored light diffused like an aurora.
- **Visual characteristics**: Soft colors, haze, and flowing movement.
- **Motion**: Gradual drift, rotation, and pulsing of gradients.
- **Use cases**: Heroes, sign-in screens, atmospheric brand backgrounds, and SaaS sites.
- **Implementation**: Multiple CSS radial gradients with `blur()` and translation animations, or WebGL.
- **Examples**: Stripe, early Vercel sites, and AI product homepages.
- **Keywords**: aurora, gradient, flow

### 2.2 Mesh Gradient
- **Definition**: Multiple color points blend into a mesh-like gradient with subtle, richly varied transitions.
- **Visual characteristics**: Interwoven colors, smooth transitions, and abstract forms.
- **Motion**: Color points drift slowly and blend as they move.
- **Use cases**: Brand visuals, posters, app backgrounds, and illustration.
- **Implementation**: A mesh gradient in Figma, SVG with filters, or a WebGL shader.
- **Examples**: Procreate assets and creative brand sites.
- **Keywords**: mesh gradient, abstraction, color blending

### 2.3 Neon Glow
- **Definition**: Highly saturated colors and strong halos evoke glowing neon tubes.
- **Visual characteristics**: Saturated colors, glowing outlines, dark backgrounds, and a cyberpunk feel.
- **Motion**: Flickering, pulsing, and electric currents in motion.
- **Use cases**: Games, trend-focused brands, nightlife and music festivals, and tech events.
- **Implementation**: Layered glows using `text-shadow` or `box-shadow` with animation.
- **Examples**: Cyberpunk websites, esports, and music festival pages.
- **Keywords**: neon, glow, cyberpunk

### 2.4 Metallic Gradient
- **Definition**: Gradients mimic the sheen of gold, silver, copper, and other metals to suggest luxury or technology.
- **Visual characteristics**: Metallic highlights, gold and silver palettes, and sharply contrasting light and dark regions.
- **Motion**: Sweeping highlights and shifting reflections.
- **Use cases**: Finance, luxury goods, medals and tier badges, and game achievements.
- **Implementation**: Linear or angled gradients with highlight masks.
- **Examples**: Finance app tier badges, luxury brand sites, and game achievement systems.
- **Keywords**: metallic, gold, luxury, sheen

### 2.5 Noise / Grain Texture
- **Definition**: A fine layer of noise adds texture and softens the harshness of large, flat color areas.
- **Visual characteristics**: Fine grain, softness, and a film-like look.
- **Motion**: Subtle flicker or shifting grain when animated.
- **Use cases**: Gradient backgrounds, illustrations, brand sites, and dark interfaces.
- **Implementation**: SVG `feTurbulence`, Canvas-generated noise, or bitmap overlays with `mix-blend-mode`.
- **Examples**: Figma, Linear, and premium websites.
- **Keywords**: noise, grain, film texture

### 2.6 Dark Mode
- **Definition**: A dark interface theme that reduces glare and can save power on OLED displays. It has become a standard product option.
- **Visual characteristics**: Dark-gray or black backgrounds, strong contrast, and adaptive contrast levels.
- **Motion**: Smooth switching between light and dark themes and adaptive colors.
- **Use cases**: Most modern apps, including developer tools, media apps, and social platforms.
- **Implementation**: CSS `prefers-color-scheme`, theme variables, or system appearance detection.
- **Examples**: Notion, Linear, Raycast, and macOS Dark Mode.
- **Keywords**: dark mode, dark theme, contrast

### 2.7 High-Contrast Brutalism
- **Definition**: Deliberately raw design uses oversized type, monochrome palettes, and heavy borders to emphasize function and rebellion.
- **Visual characteristics**: Large type, black and white or highly saturated colors, hard borders, and little ornament.
- **Motion**: Abrupt motion, flicker, and jitter, deliberately left unpolished.
- **Use cases**: Creative studios, personal brands, street culture, and independent publications.
- **Implementation**: CSS alone, with the emphasis on typography and color.
- **Examples**: Some independent designer sites, streetwear brands, and cultural publications.
- **Keywords**: brutalism, rebellion, high contrast, raw

## Aesthetic Styles

> These visual styles evoke a particular culture or era and are often paired with motion.

### 3.1 Glitch Effect
- **Definition**: Offset, tearing, and RGB separation effects imitate digital signal errors.
- **Visual characteristics**: Separated RGB channels, misalignment, tearing, and static.
- **Motion**: Flickering, jitter, and abrupt jumps out of alignment.
- **Use cases**: Musician sites, trend-focused brands, games, and experimental work.
- **Implementation**: CSS `clip-path` offsets with animated RGB separation, or Canvas.
- **Examples**: Music festival sites and experimental design projects.
- **Keywords**: glitch, RGB separation, offset, digital distortion

### 3.2 Pixel Art
- **Definition**: A retro style that recalls the graphics of 8-bit and 16-bit games.
- **Visual characteristics**: Visible pixel blocks, limited palettes, and retro game visuals.
- **Motion**: Frame-by-frame pixel animation and retro UI feedback.
- **Use cases**: Indie games, retro themes, developer websites, and playful marketing.
- **Implementation**: CSS `image-rendering: pixelated` with frame-by-frame animations.
- **Examples**: Retro games and pixel-art websites.
- **Keywords**: pixel art, 8-bit, retro, games

### 3.3 Y2K / Vaporwave
- **Definition**: Turn-of-the-millennium tech optimism meets retro electronic aesthetics, mixing metallic and plastic finishes, gradients, and early-web motifs.
- **Visual characteristics**: Pink, purple, and blue gradients, metallic and plastic textures, and retro web elements.
- **Motion**: Flicker, rotation, and dreamy drifting.
- **Use cases**: Trend-focused brands, music, retro themes, and products rooted in Gen Z culture.
- **Implementation**: CSS gradients, bitmap assets, and retro motion.
- **Examples**: Trend-focused brands and vaporwave music visuals.
- **Keywords**: Y2K, vaporwave, retrofuturism, millennium

### 3.4 Memphis / Pop
- **Definition**: A playful, pop-art-inspired style built around bold geometry and clashing saturated colors.
- **Visual characteristics**: Geometric shapes, high-contrast colors, polka dots, and stripes.
- **Motion**: Bounces, rotations, and staggered entrances.
- **Use cases**: Creative brands, education, events, and products aimed at younger audiences.
- **Implementation**: CSS shapes and animation.
- **Examples**: Creative event posters and youth-oriented brand sites.
- **Keywords**: Memphis, pop art, color blocking

### 3.5 Minimalism
- **Definition**: A pared-down style built on whitespace, restraint, and order.
- **Visual characteristics**: Ample whitespace, a single accent color, and a clear hierarchy.
- **Motion**: Restrained, slow, and precise transitions.
- **Use cases**: Premium brands, luxury goods, SaaS, and content platforms.
- **Implementation**: CSS alone, with careful typography and spacing.
- **Examples**: Apple, Muji, Linear, and other premium brands.
- **Keywords**: minimalism, whitespace, restraint, premium

### 3.6 Glasspunk / Futuristic Transparency
- **Definition**: Futuristic visuals use expansive transparent surfaces, holographic effects, and iridescent reflections.
- **Visual characteristics**: Holographic iridescence, overlapping transparent layers, and futuristic lighting.
- **Motion**: Flowing iridescence and sweeping light.
- **Use cases**: AR and VR, AI products, tech launches, and conceptual interfaces.
- **Implementation**: Gradient overlays, blend modes, and animation.
- **Examples**: Vision Pro interface concepts and promotional material for AI products.
- **Keywords**: futuristic, transparent, holographic, iridescent

## Microinteractions and Motion

> Small, precise animations make an interface feel responsive and alive.

### 4.1 Microinteractions
- **Definition**: Immediate, precise feedback for a single action such as a click, hover, or swipe.
- **Visual characteristics**: Small changes to individual elements.
- **Motion**: Press states, bounces, slight icon rotations, like bursts, and switch transitions.
- **Use cases**: Interactive components such as buttons, forms, tabs, bookmarks, and likes.
- **Implementation**: CSS transitions, Framer Motion, or Lottie.
- **Examples**: Twitter likes, iOS switches, and Airbnb saves.
- **Keywords**: microinteractions, feedback, details, bounce

### 4.2 Skeleton Screen
- **Definition**: Gray placeholders preview the page structure before content loads, replacing a conventional spinner.
- **Visual characteristics**: Gray gradient placeholders shaped like the incoming content.
- **Motion**: A shimmer sweeps across the placeholders.
- **Use cases**: Loading lists, cards, and detail pages.
- **Implementation**: A CSS gradient with a shimmer animation.
- **Examples**: Facebook, LinkedIn, Zhihu, and many other modern apps.
- **Keywords**: skeleton screen, loading, placeholders, shimmer

### 4.3 Creative Loading
- **Definition**: A branded or playful loading animation makes waiting feel less frustrating.
- **Visual characteristics**: Brand elements, playful graphics, and progress feedback.
- **Motion**: Loops, progress updates, and a finishing transition.
- **Use cases**: Splash screens, game loading screens, and brand sites.
- **Implementation**: Lottie, SVG, Canvas, or CSS.
- **Examples**: Google products, game loading screens, and branded splash screens.
- **Keywords**: loading, splash screen, progress, branding

### 4.4 Page Transition
- **Definition**: An animation preserves a sense of continuity when switching between pages or views.
- **Visual characteristics**: Slides, fades, scaling, and overlays.
- **Motion**: Smooth movement with a clear sense of direction and layering.
- **Use cases**: App navigation, SPA view changes, and mobile routing.
- **Implementation**: CSS transitions, Vue Transition, Framer Motion, or React Navigation.
- **Examples**: iOS navigation and various mobile apps.
- **Keywords**: transition, page change, navigation

### 4.5 Shared Element Transition
- **Definition**: The same element moves continuously between two views to show their relationship, for example when a list thumbnail expands into a detail image.
- **Visual characteristics**: An element transforms continuously across views.
- **Motion**: An element moves, grows, or shrinks without a visible break.
- **Use cases**: List-to-detail navigation, image browsing, and search-to-results transitions.
- **Implementation**: Flutter Hero, Android Shared Element transitions, or React Native libraries.
- **Examples**: Google Play, Taobao product details, and Pinterest.
- **Keywords**: shared element, hero, transition, continuity

### 4.6 Spring Animation
- **Definition**: A motion curve models the rebound and damping of a real spring to make movement feel natural.
- **Visual characteristics**: Natural trajectories with a visible rebound.
- **Motion**: Overshoot, damped oscillation, and motion that follows a drag gesture.
- **Use cases**: Draggable elements, dialogs, switches, and moving cards.
- **Implementation**: Framer Motion `spring`, React Spring, or SwiftUI springs.
- **Examples**: iOS dialogs, Framer drag interactions, and Material Design.
- **Keywords**: spring, physics, rebound, damping

### 4.7 Gooey Effect
- **Definition**: Nearby elements stretch, merge, and separate like droplets of a viscous liquid.
- **Visual characteristics**: Smooth, elastic forms that merge like liquid.
- **Motion**: Merging, separation, and sticky stretching.
- **Use cases**: Expanding player controls, menu variants, logo morphs, and loading.
- **Implementation**: SVG `filter: gooey` using Gaussian blur and boosted contrast.
- **Examples**: Dribbble concepts and media player interfaces.
- **Keywords**: fluid, viscous, gooey, merging, morphing

### 4.8 Particle Effect
- **Definition**: Many tiny particles combine to form a moving visual effect.
- **Visual characteristics**: Particle swarms, glowing points, stardust, and sparks.
- **Motion**: Drifting, clustering and dispersing, trails, and bursts.
- **Use cases**: Brand backgrounds, event pages, games, and data visualization.
- **Implementation**: Canvas, WebGL, Particle.js, or Three.js.
- **Examples**: Sites with particle backgrounds, branded event pages, and game effects.
- **Keywords**: particles, stardust, Canvas, WebGL

### 4.9 Lottie Vector Animation
- **Definition**: A cross-platform vector animation described by JSON, exported from After Effects using Bodymovin.
- **Visual characteristics**: Scalable vectors without loss of quality.
- **Motion**: Complex tweening, path animation, and controllable playback progress.
- **Use cases**: Brand motion, animated icons, empty states, loading, and onboarding.
- **Implementation**: A Lottie library renders JSON on Web, iOS, Android, or React Native.
- **Examples**: App onboarding screens and animated icons.
- **Keywords**: Lottie, vector animation, JSON, After Effects, cross-platform

### 4.10 Ripple Effect
- **Definition**: A ripple spreads from the point of contact on tap or click, a hallmark of Material Design.
- **Visual characteristics**: An expanding circular ripple.
- **Motion**: The ripple radiates from the contact point and fades.
- **Use cases**: Click feedback on buttons, list items, and cards.
- **Implementation**: Position a circle with CSS or JavaScript, then scale and fade it.
- **Examples**: Material Design components.
- **Keywords**: ripple, click feedback, expansion, Material Design

## Scroll-Driven Motion

> Scrolling acts as an animation timeline for storytelling and immersive browsing.

### 5.1 Parallax Scrolling
- **Definition**: Foreground and background move at different speeds, creating a sense of depth.
- **Visual characteristics**: Multiple layers move at different speeds.
- **Motion**: Layers shift relative to one another as the user scrolls.
- **Use cases**: Heroes, long-form narrative pages, and brand websites.
- **Implementation**: GSAP, scroll-driven CSS transforms, or Lenis.
- **Examples**: Apple product pages and award-winning websites.
- **Keywords**: parallax, depth, offset, scrolling

### 5.2 Scrollytelling
- **Definition**: Scrolling advances the story, letting readers discover it at their own pace.
- **Visual characteristics**: Content unfolds and scenes change as the user scrolls.
- **Motion**: Chapter progression, animations in pinned sections, and frame-by-frame playback.
- **Use cases**: Brand stories, product overviews, data journalism, and news features.
- **Implementation**: GSAP ScrollTrigger, Framer Motion `useScroll`, or ScrollMagic.
- **Examples**: New York Times features, Apple launch pages, and ESPN data journalism.
- **Keywords**: scrollytelling, narrative, immersion, ScrollTrigger

### 5.3 Scroll Reveal
- **Definition**: Elements fade in, rise, or unfold in sequence as they enter the viewport.
- **Visual characteristics**: Elements appear in a staggered sequence.
- **Motion**: Fades, upward movement, scaling, and staggered delays.
- **Use cases**: Lists, cards, section headings, and portfolios.
- **Implementation**: IntersectionObserver, AOS, or Framer Motion `whileInView`.
- **Examples**: Portfolios and lists on official websites.
- **Keywords**: scroll reveal, viewport, fade-in, AOS

### 5.4 Smooth Scrolling
- **Definition**: Scrolling follows input with fluid inertia rather than abrupt jumps, as with Lenis interpolation.
- **Visual characteristics**: Fluid, uninterrupted scrolling.
- **Motion**: Inertial motion and eased tracking.
- **Use cases**: Premium sites, portfolios, and long pages.
- **Implementation**: Lenis, GSAP ScrollSmoother, or Locomotive Scroll.
- **Examples**: Common among Awwwards-winning websites.
- **Keywords**: smooth scrolling, Lenis, inertia, easing

### 5.5 Scroll Snap
- **Definition**: When scrolling ends, the viewport settles at a defined position for panel-by-panel or page-by-page browsing.
- **Visual characteristics**: Fullscreen sections browsed one screen at a time.
- **Motion**: Snapping into place and switching between panels.
- **Use cases**: Fullscreen product showcases, galleries, and mobile panels.
- **Implementation**: CSS `scroll-snap-type` and `scroll-snap-align`.
- **Examples**: Apple product panels and fullscreen gallery sites.
- **Keywords**: scroll snap, panels, paging, fullscreen

### 5.6 Horizontal Scrolling
- **Definition**: A sideways scrolling layout for galleries, timelines, or rows of products.
- **Visual characteristics**: Horizontal arrangement and displacement.
- **Motion**: Sideways translation, sometimes linked to vertical scroll.
- **Use cases**: Product ranges, galleries, timelines, and brand case studies.
- **Implementation**: GSAP horizontal translation tied to vertical scrolling.
- **Examples**: Branded case-study showcases and horizontal portfolio galleries.
- **Keywords**: horizontal scrolling, galleries, timelines

### 5.7 Infinite Scrolling
- **Definition**: More content loads automatically near the end of the list and is appended without a page break.
- **Visual characteristics**: Additional content keeps appearing at the bottom.
- **Motion**: Loading indicators and a seamless continuation.
- **Use cases**: Content feeds, product listings, and social feeds.
- **Implementation**: IntersectionObserver and a paginated API.
- **Examples**: Instagram, Twitter, Taobao, and Douyin.
- **Keywords**: infinite scroll, load more, feeds

### 5.8 Scroll Progress Indicator
- **Definition**: A reading-progress indicator appears at the top or side of a page.
- **Visual characteristics**: A progress bar or circular indicator.
- **Motion**: Fills in real time as the user scrolls.
- **Use cases**: Long reads, tutorials, and blogs.
- **Implementation**: Listen for scroll events and calculate progress.
- **Examples**: Medium, technical blogs, and long-form reading pages.
- **Keywords**: progress bar, reading progress, scrolling

## 3D and Spatial Design

> A third dimension gives the interface a sense of space, depth, and immersion.

### 6.1 Real-Time 3D Rendering (WebGL / Three.js)
- **Definition**: Render interactive 3D scenes and objects in real time in the browser or app.
- **Visual characteristics**: Lifelike lighting and shadows, 3D models, and interactive scenes.
- **Motion**: Live rotation, scaling, physics, and shader animations.
- **Use cases**: Product showcases, games, brand sites, and visualization.
- **Implementation**: Three.js, React Three Fiber, Babylon.js, or WebGL.
- **Examples**: 3D brand sites and award-winning WebGL work.
- **Keywords**: 3D, WebGL, Three.js, real-time rendering, models

### 6.2 360° Product View
- **Definition**: An interactive product model can be rotated by dragging, zoomed, or taken apart.
- **Visual characteristics**: A rotatable three-dimensional view of the product.
- **Motion**: Drag-to-rotate, zoom, and exploded-view animations.
- **Use cases**: E-commerce, hardware sites, automotive, and consumer electronics.
- **Implementation**: Three.js, Spline, or an image sequence.
- **Examples**: Apple product pages, automotive sites, and 3D e-commerce displays.
- **Keywords**: 360°, product view, rotation, exploded view, e-commerce

### 6.3 Simulated 3D Tilt (3D Hover)
- **Definition**: An element tilts in perspective in response to the pointer or gyroscope, suggesting depth without a real 3D model.
- **Visual characteristics**: Cards tilt in perspective as they follow the pointer.
- **Motion**: Perspective rotation, tracking, and return to neutral.
- **Use cases**: Cards, payment cards, posters, and portfolios.
- **Implementation**: CSS transforms with perspective and JavaScript pointer tracking, or Tilt.js.
- **Examples**: Tilting payment cards and portfolio cards.
- **Keywords**: tilt, perspective, tracking, 3D hover

### 6.4 Spatial UI
- **Definition**: Interface elements occupy three-dimensional space in floating glass layers, as in Vision Pro interactions.
- **Visual characteristics**: Floating glass layers, spatial depth, and translucent lighting.
- **Motion**: Floating elements, movement through space, and parallax depth.
- **Use cases**: XR devices, conceptual interfaces, and futuristic products.
- **Implementation**: SwiftUI for visionOS, a 3D engine, or multilayer parallax.
- **Examples**: Apple Vision Pro and visionOS.
- **Keywords**: spatial UI, visionOS, floating, XR

### 6.5 AR / VR Interface
- **Definition**: An interface for augmented or virtual reality integrates controls with the real or virtual world.
- **Visual characteristics**: Blends physical and digital elements and anchors controls in space.
- **Motion**: Spatial tracking, gesture interaction, and environmental responses.
- **Use cases**: AR try-ons, VR apps, games, and education.
- **Implementation**: ARKit, ARCore, WebXR, or Unity.
- **Examples**: IKEA AR furniture placement, AR makeup try-ons, and VR apps.
- **Keywords**: AR, VR, augmented reality, virtual reality, WebXR

### 6.6 Depth Layering
- **Definition**: Shadows, blur, and stacking create the impression of cards floating above the content.
- **Visual characteristics**: Distinct layers and a sense of suspension.
- **Motion**: Stacked reveals and sliding layers.
- **Use cases**: Dialogs, menus, floating cards, and mobile navigation.
- **Implementation**: z-index, shadows, and backdrop blur.
- **Examples**: iOS dialogs and floating-card designs.
- **Keywords**: depth, layering, floating surfaces, stacking

## Typographic Motion

> Type itself becomes a moving visual element rather than a static composition.

### 7.1 Kinetic Typography
- **Definition**: Text moves, changes shape, or changes color to become the focal point.
- **Visual characteristics**: Oversized bold type that changes over time.
- **Motion**: Weight, spacing, or color changes on scroll; individual characters animate.
- **Use cases**: Website heroes, event pages, brand manifestos, and video openings.
- **Implementation**: Variable fonts with scroll tracking, or character-by-character GSAP animation.
- **Examples**: Apple product pages and oversized headings on creative sites.
- **Keywords**: kinetic typography, animated text, character animation

### 7.2 Text Reveal
- **Definition**: Masks, clipping, or character-by-character timing progressively reveal text.
- **Visual characteristics**: Text emerges from behind a mask.
- **Motion**: Character or line fades, upward slides, and clipped reveals.
- **Use cases**: Headlines, openings, and section titles.
- **Implementation**: CSS `clip-path`, overflow masks, or GSAP SplitText.
- **Examples**: Headlines on award-winning sites and brand openings.
- **Keywords**: text reveal, masking, character animation, SplitText

### 7.3 Variable Fonts
- **Definition**: A single font file supports continuous changes along axes such as weight, width, and italics.
- **Visual characteristics**: Smooth changes in weight or width.
- **Motion**: Font weight shifts continuously in response to scrolling or interaction.
- **Use cases**: Animated headings, responsive typography, and branding.
- **Implementation**: CSS `font-variation-settings` with animation.
- **Examples**: Dynamic headlines on creative websites.
- **Keywords**: variable fonts, weight, width

### 7.4 Typewriter Effect
- **Definition**: Characters appear one at a time as if typed, often with a blinking cursor.
- **Visual characteristics**: Sequential character reveals and a blinking cursor.
- **Motion**: Character-by-character output and cursor blinking, optionally on a loop.
- **Use cases**: Terminal-inspired designs, intros, personal sites, and command-line animations.
- **Implementation**: Character-by-character JavaScript output or CSS `steps()`.
- **Examples**: Developer sites and terminal-themed websites.
- **Keywords**: typewriter, character reveal, terminal, cursor

### 7.5 Oversized Bold Typography
- **Definition**: An oversized, bold headline takes the place of a traditional hero image as the page’s main visual.
- **Visual characteristics**: Dominant type at a very large size and heavy weight.
- **Motion**: Entrances, scroll-driven scaling, and staggered reveals.
- **Use cases**: Website heroes, event pages, and brand manifestos.
- **Implementation**: Responsive sizing with CSS `clamp()` and animation.
- **Examples**: Creative websites and event landing pages.
- **Keywords**: oversized type, bold, typography, hero, headline

## Layout and Structure

> The layout helps define the interface’s visual style and experience.

### 8.1 Bento Grid
- **Definition**: Rounded cards of different sizes fit together like compartments in a Japanese bento box: orderly but playful.
- **Visual characteristics**: Rounded modular cards and high information density.
- **Motion**: Card entrances and expansion on hover.
- **Use cases**: Feature showcases, portfolios, data summaries, and app overviews.
- **Implementation**: CSS Grid with rounded cards.
- **Examples**: Apple Keynote event pages and product sites.
- **Keywords**: Bento Grid, modularity, cards, grid

### 8.2 Card-Based UI
- **Definition**: Individual cards group related information into clearly bounded, scannable units.
- **Visual characteristics**: Rounded cards, shadows, and clear separation.
- **Motion**: Card hover, expansion, and flip effects.
- **Use cases**: Content aggregation, e-commerce, news, and settings pages.
- **Implementation**: CSS and card components.
- **Examples**: Pinterest, Airbnb, and various feeds.
- **Keywords**: card UI, content aggregation, rounded corners

### 8.3 Immersive Edge-to-Edge Layout
- **Definition**: Borderless content extends to the screen edges to create immersion.
- **Visual characteristics**: Fullscreen content, no visible boundaries, and visuals that extend to the edges.
- **Motion**: Fullscreen transitions and immersive scrolling.
- **Use cases**: Media, games, content consumption, and mobile interfaces.
- **Implementation**: Remove CSS padding and use a fullscreen layout.
- **Examples**: iOS 26 and immersive media apps.
- **Keywords**: edge-to-edge, borderless, immersion, fullscreen, full bleed

### 8.4 Broken Grid / Asymmetry
- **Definition**: Deliberate breaks in grid alignment use asymmetry, offsets, and overlap to create tension and character.
- **Visual characteristics**: Asymmetry, offsets, overlap, and a sense of movement.
- **Motion**: Offset slide animations and interactions between overlapping elements.
- **Use cases**: Creative portfolios, fashion brands, and art websites.
- **Implementation**: Offset CSS Grid or Flexbox placement, negative margins, and overlaps.
- **Examples**: Fashion brand sites and creative studios.
- **Keywords**: broken grid, asymmetry, offset, overlap

### 8.5 Masonry Layout
- **Definition**: Columns pack items of varying heights without forcing them into aligned rows.
- **Visual characteristics**: A tight arrangement of staggered columns.
- **Motion**: Loading on scroll and staggered card entrances.
- **Use cases**: Image communities, galleries, and e-commerce product walls.
- **Implementation**: CSS columns or a JavaScript masonry library.
- **Examples**: Pinterest, Huaban, and image sites.
- **Keywords**: masonry, image wall, staggered, multi-column

## Cursor and Gesture Interaction

> Inputs such as the pointer, gestures, and voice become part of the design.

### 9.1 Custom Cursor
- **Definition**: The pointer changes into a magnetic dot, glowing trail, magnifier, or another graphic tied to the brand.
- **Visual characteristics**: Custom cursor shapes, trails, and halos.
- **Motion**: Smooth tracking via interpolation, magnetic attraction, and shape changes.
- **Use cases**: Personal portfolios, creative studios, and e-commerce detail pages.
- **Implementation**: JavaScript `mousemove` events and linear interpolation (lerp).
- **Examples**: Common on creative sites featured by Awwwards.
- **Keywords**: cursor, tracking, magnetic effect, trail

### 9.2 Magnetic Effect
- **Definition**: Elements appear to attract or stretch toward a nearby pointer or finger.
- **Visual characteristics**: Elements shift toward the pointer.
- **Motion**: Attraction, stretching, and rebound.
- **Use cases**: Buttons, icons, cards, and navigation.
- **Implementation**: Calculate distance in JavaScript and offset the element with a transform.
- **Examples**: Creative website buttons and magnetic icons.
- **Keywords**: magnetic effect, attraction, stretching, physics

### 9.3 Hover Effect
- **Definition**: An element gives visual or animated feedback when the pointer hovers over it.
- **Visual characteristics**: Changes in color, shadow, or scale, or a reveal of images and text.
- **Motion**: Scaling, color changes, expansion, and image previews.
- **Use cases**: Cards, buttons, images, navigation, and products.
- **Implementation**: CSS `:hover` with transitions.
- **Examples**: E-commerce product cards, navigation menus, and other cards.
- **Keywords**: hover, feedback, preview, transition

### 9.4 Drag and Drop
- **Definition**: Users drag elements to move, reorder, and place them.
- **Visual characteristics**: Drag previews and placeholder cues.
- **Motion**: The element follows the pointer, items animate into their new order, and the drop gets feedback.
- **Use cases**: Task boards, file management, form reordering, and editors.
- **Implementation**: dnd-kit, SortableJS, or Framer Motion drag.
- **Examples**: Trello, Notion, and cloud storage apps.
- **Keywords**: drag and drop, reordering, task board

### 9.5 Gesture Interaction
- **Definition**: Gestures such as swipe, pinch, long press, and edge swipe control the interface.
- **Visual characteristics**: The interface changes in response to gestures.
- **Motion**: Content follows the finger; swipes, pinches, and long-press menus provide direct control.
- **Use cases**: Mobile navigation, image browsing, video, and games.
- **Implementation**: Hammer.js, React Native Gesture Handler, or native iOS gestures.
- **Examples**: Mobile back gestures and pinch-to-zoom on images.
- **Keywords**: gestures, swipe, pinch, touch

### 9.6 Zero UI / Voice
- **Definition**: Voice, gestures, or automation replace some traditional on-screen controls with less visible interactions.
- **Visual characteristics**: A minimal interface or a voice waveform.
- **Motion**: Animated voice waveforms and command feedback.
- **Use cases**: Voice assistants, smart homes, in-car interfaces, and accessibility.
- **Implementation**: A speech recognition API with waveform animation.
- **Examples**: Siri, Google Assistant, and smart speaker interfaces.
- **Keywords**: zero UI, voice, invisible interaction

## Data Visualization Motion

> Motion can make data easier to understand and show the story behind it.

### 10.1 Count-Up Animation
- **Definition**: A number animates from zero or another starting value to a target value.
- **Visual characteristics**: Increasing digits and visible unit labels.
- **Motion**: Animated counting that eases to a stop.
- **Use cases**: Data displays, KPIs, achievement pages, and landing pages.
- **Implementation**: CountUp.js or a JavaScript easing loop.
- **Examples**: Data dashboards and product metrics displays.
- **Keywords**: count-up, counting, metrics, easing

### 10.2 Chart Animation
- **Definition**: Bar, line, and pie charts animate when entering, transitioning, or updating live.
- **Visual characteristics**: Charts draw dynamically.
- **Motion**: Bars grow, lines trace, pie slices unfold, and data changes animate.
- **Use cases**: Large data displays, BI dashboards, and reports.
- **Implementation**: ECharts, Chart.js, D3.js, or Recharts.
- **Examples**: BI tools and large-screen data displays.
- **Keywords**: charts, data visualization, ECharts, D3

### 10.3 Data Storytelling
- **Definition**: Scroll and animation guide readers through the story behind the data, one step at a time.
- **Visual characteristics**: Data visualization combined with narrative.
- **Motion**: Scroll-driven data changes and highlights on key points.
- **Use cases**: Data journalism, annual reports, and news features.
- **Implementation**: D3.js with ScrollTrigger, or Flourish.
- **Examples**: New York Times data journalism and annual report pages.
- **Keywords**: data storytelling, journalism, scrolling, D3

## AI and Generative Interfaces

> AI generates or adapts the interface in real time to suit the individual user.

### 11.1 Generative UI
- **Definition**: AI generates or rearranges the interface in real time based on user intent instead of relying on a hard-coded layout.
- **Visual characteristics**: Dynamically generated components and layouts.
- **Motion**: Components stream into view and transition as the layout changes.
- **Use cases**: AI assistants, smart forms, and conversational interfaces.
- **Implementation**: An LLM with a component rendering framework such as the Vercel AI SDK.
- **Examples**: Interactive components in ChatGPT and Claude, and AI customer support.
- **Keywords**: generative UI, AI, dynamic generation, streaming

### 11.2 Adaptive / Personalized UI
- **Definition**: The interface automatically adjusts layout, colors, and content based on behavior, preferences, and context.
- **Visual characteristics**: Personalized layouts and content.
- **Motion**: Smooth layout reorganization and theme switching.
- **Use cases**: Content recommendations, personalized homepages, and adaptive themes.
- **Implementation**: User behavior data with a dynamic layout engine.
- **Examples**: Netflix, Spotify, and personalized news apps.
- **Keywords**: adaptive, personalized, recommendations

### 11.3 AI-Generated Motion / Real-Time Rendering
- **Definition**: AI generates or helps create motion, 3D assets, and video transitions.
- **Visual characteristics**: High-quality AI-generated visual and motion assets.
- **Motion**: Generated animations, assisted tweening, and style transfer.
- **Use cases**: Prototyping, video production, game assets, and creative production.
- **Implementation**: Generative models with motion tools such as Sora, Runway, or Luma.
- **Examples**: AI video generation and AI prototyping tools.
- **Keywords**: AI motion, generation, video, tweening, style transfer
